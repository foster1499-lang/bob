# Resume pressure test - Indeed version (2026-10-02)

Source: the resume on your Indeed profile, pulled through the Indeed connector. Your real Word/PDF file may be better. If it is, send it to me and I'll test that one too.

**Verdict: this resume won't get you past a recruiter for a $75k+ underwriter job yet.** The experience is real and good. The page just doesn't show it. Everything below can be fixed in one session.

## Will hurt you (fix before you apply anywhere)

1. **No bullets under any job.** Recruiters see three job titles and nothing else. They spend about 7 seconds on a resume. There's nothing for them to grab.
2. **The State Farm job has no title.** It reads "ERIK GUILLORY STATE FARM (JANUARY 2020)". That's 4+ years of your homeowners experience with no label on it.
3. **No end dates.** Farmers shows "September 2024" with no end date, so it looks like you still work there. A background check will catch that. Add real dates in month and year format.
4. **"Underwriter" is never your job title.** You're applying for underwriter jobs, but your titles are Placement Specialist, (blank), and HSE Coordinator. Hiring software and recruiters match on titles. If you made underwriting calls (accepting, declining, setting terms or price), the bullets have to say that plainly.
5. **The skills block reads like keyword stuffing.** Lines like "Brokerage Placement Carrier Guidelines, P&C Underwriting Risk Selection & Evaluation" run several skills together. Software reads that badly, and people notice it.

## Risky (be ready to back up every one, or cut it)

6. **Claims a sharp interviewer will test:** "Lloyd's Market", "National Account Underwriting", "High Net Worth Underwriting", "Middle-Market Underwriting", "Multiline Underwriting". Trium writes on Lloyd's paper, and they *will* ask. If you can't tell a 2-minute story about each one, cut it.
7. **Phrases that look like AI-training job keywords:** "Underwriting Scenarios", "Underwriting Assessments", "Written Feedback", "Written Reasoning", "Technical Accuracy". They don't belong on a carrier resume. But see the secret below. They're perfect for a second resume.

## Easy wins

8. **No summary at the top.** Add 2-3 lines: who you are, your years, your lane, and that you want remote work.
9. **Education:** fix the capital "S" in "Southern" and add your major. Leave the year off if you'd rather.
10. **Licenses:** add the state and "Active". For example: "Louisiana Property & Casualty Producer License, Active."
11. **No designations.** Start the **AINS** (Associate in General Insurance, from The Institutes) and list it as "AINS, in progress". It costs a few hundred dollars and can be done in weeks. Keep the CPCU for later.
12. **HSE Coordinator:** keep it to one line. Spin it as hazard spotting and loss control. That's real underwriting value.

## Indeed profile settings that cost you jobs

- **Max commute: 15 minutes.** This can hide remote and hybrid jobs from you. Set it to remote, or 60 minutes.
- **Preferred title "Project Supervisor".** That's off target. Change it to "Property Underwriter" or "Personal Lines Underwriter".
- **Years of experience:** say "6+ years" on the resume. Don't write "10+" anywhere.

## The secret

Your "written reasoning / underwriting scenarios" skills fit a whole job market most underwriters don't know about. **AI companies pay insurance experts by the hour to write and grade underwriting scenarios that train their models.** It's remote, often $40-$100+/hr, and you can start in days, not months. Keep a second, short resume tuned for those platforms. It's bridge income while the full-time search runs, and it doesn't conflict with it.
